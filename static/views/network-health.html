        What's on the OLT

        What it is

        The OLT (Huawei MA5680T) is the fibre head-end. Our fibre customers hang off its PON ports: each port feeds one fibre that passive splitters share between many ONTs, the little boxes in customers' homes and at hotspot sites. Names on the OLT are the customer's JS code or the site name.

        ONT → splitter → PON port → OLT → CRS326 (sfp-sfpplus3) → the routers → internet

        The CCR2116 reads the OLT over SNMP every 5 minutes; the CCR2116 and CCR2004 also report their PPPoE sessions (home customers on the CCR2116, hotspot sites on the CCR2004).

        What we read

        • The OLT's uplink: frames arriving damaged (CRC %) from the CRS326, restarts, and whether the OLT still answers.
        • Each PON: how many ONTs are online and offline.
        • Each ONT: online or offline, the light each way, distance in metres, and why it last went down.
        • PPPoE: which sessions are up on each router, matched to the ONTs by JS code or site name.

        Reading the light

        Two numbers per ONT, in dBm (closer to zero is stronger):

        • ONT receives (downstream): weak under , bad under , overloaded over .
        • OLT receives (upstream): weak under , bad under .
        • Both weak, on several ONTs of one PON: shared fibre or a splitter.
        • Downstream fine, upstream bad: that ONT's transmitter or connector.
        • Overloaded: too much light, fit an attenuator.

        Why an ONT went down

        • LOSi / LOBi: it lost light. A cut or dirty fibre, or a bad connector.
        • Dying gasp: it lost power and said so. A power cut at the customer.
        • Reset: it rebooted.

        What we look for

        • A fibre fault: or more ONTs on one PON down together, mostly with light loss. Serious, texted.
        • A power cut: the same, mostly dying gasp. Warning, texted.
        • A whole PON down: every online ONT on it (at least ) gone at once. Serious, texted.
        • One ONT with light loss: a warning on the page. One dying gasp is only listed.
        • Light getting worse: an ONT's last 24 h against its usual week, dB weaker: a warning. or more ONTs of one PON dB weaker: texted.
        • A hotspot site's PPPoE session missing for readings in a row: texted. % of a router's sessions gone at once (of at least ): texted.
        • Line up, no internet: an ONT online but its PPPoE session missing for readings in a row: a warning.
        • The OLT uplink: damaged frames over % twice in a row, the port going down, a restart, or no SNMP answer for min.
        • Likely same splitter: ONTs within m of each other, or that went down together times or more.
        • Stale registrations: on the OLT but never online since first seen. Listed, never alerted.

        "Texted" means an SMS to the alert numbers, which only goes out when SMS is switched to live (log-only until then). Everything else is on the page.

        Where to look

        • OLT > PON: every PON port with its ONUs and SmartOLT's own signal grades; a row opens its ONUs.
        • Incidents: site visits, from what SmartOLT reports, then every incident.
        • Inside network: the OLT's uplink and restarts.
